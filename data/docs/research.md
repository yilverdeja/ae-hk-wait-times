## Research Findings: Hong Kong A&E Wait Times (May 2018 – May 2026)

### [2025] - Misuse of Accident and Emergency Services in Hong Kong Public Hospitals
* **Authors/Institution:** (Book chapter authors), Springer Nature
* **Type:** Published Peer-Reviewed Book Chapter
* **Link:** https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-96-0817-1_14
* **Core Analysis:** This chapter explores the misuse of accident and emergency (A&E) services in Hong Kong, finding that the main reasons for people to misuse A&E services include weaknesses of alternative services, low public awareness, and financial considerations.[[1]](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-96-0817-1_14)
* **Relevance to Cat 4/5 & Data Presentation:**
    - The consequences of misuse bring challenges to service quality, patient experience, and waste of resources. The government is recommended to establish a more comprehensive primary healthcare system, empower people through public education, and narrow the charge gap between A&E services and private primary healthcare to encourage visits to private providers.[[1]](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-96-0817-1_14)
    - A public-facing tool could integrate links to nearby private GP/clinic alternatives (with hours and estimated fees) alongside real-time wait times, particularly when Cat 4/5 waits exceed a certain threshold.

### [January 2025] - Emergency Medicine Transformation to Improve Hospital Admissions and Patient Outcomes: Emergency Ambulatory Care Model in Hong Kong
* **Authors/Institution:** Shin Yan Man, Kwong Hang Yeung et al., Accident & Emergency Medicine Academic Unit, CUHK / Prince of Wales Hospital
* **Type:** Published Peer-Reviewed Paper
* **Link:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hkj2.70001
* **Core Analysis:** The Prince of Wales Hospital's Emergency Ambulatory Care (EAC) service, initiated in August 2021, is an innovative approach to ED overcrowding. This study evaluates the impact of EAC service on hospital admissions, focusing on patients with ambulatory care-sensitive conditions over the initial 5 months.[[1]](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hkj2.70001)
* **Relevance to Cat 4/5 & Data Presentation:**
    - A total of 408 patients were recruited and the service achieved a high discharge rate of 89.5%, contributing to a 7.5% reduction in hospital admissions for medical conditions.[[1]](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hkj2.70001) This demonstrates the viability of same-day ambulatory pathways that could be promoted to Cat 4/5 patients waiting in the queue.
    - A public-facing dashboard could flag when EAC-type services are available and direct eligible low-acuity patients, reducing their contribution to overall ED wait times.

### [December 2023, published online] - Patient Sensitivity to Emergency Department Waiting Time Announcements
* **Authors/Institution:** Eric Park (Wake Forest University), Huiyin Ouyang (HKU), Jingqi Wang (CUHK-Shenzhen), Sergei Savin, Siu Chung Leung, Timothy H. Rainer
* **Type:** Published Peer-Reviewed Paper
* **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1287/msom.2022.0457
* **Core Analysis:** The study analyzed over 1.3 million patient visits to the 17 tier-one public EDs. It structurally estimated the fraction of patients sensitive to the announced WT and their sensitivity. In the patient's ED choice decision, they estimated the trade-off between travel distance and expected WT. They found that 3.1% of patients are sensitive to the announced WT and are willing to travel an additional 4.8 km to save one hour of waiting.[[3]](https://sfi.cuhk.edu.cn/en/show-51-1382.html)
* **Relevance to Cat 4/5 & Data Presentation:**
    - **This is the single most directly relevant paper to the stated goal.** Urgent patients are less likely to be sensitive to the delay announcement than less urgent patients, but those that are sensitive are more WT averse than their less urgent counterparts.[[4]](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/msom.2022.0457) This means Cat 4/5 patients are the most likely to use wait-time data to change behaviour — if the data is presented effectively.
    - Counterfactual analysis shows that the average actual WT and number of patients who leave without being seen can be reduced by 4.6% and 8.5%, respectively, by increasing the fraction of sensitive patients to 15.0% and reducing the announced WT assessment window to one hour from the current three hours. Further improvement can be achieved by providing predicted WT information based on the current level of ED crowding or less extreme past performance—median WT rather than the currently used 95th percentile.[[4]](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/msom.2022.0457)
    - To promote the ED WT announcement system and increase awareness, the Hong Kong government should focus on the older population and Kowloon district.[[9]](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/SSRN_ID4492043_code2131244.pdf?abstractid=4047005&mirid=1)
    - **Key Insight:** The current system reports the 95th percentile wait time over a 3-hour window, which is overly pessimistic and stale. Switching to a **1-hour median predicted WT** would make the data actionable for Cat 4/5 patients.

### [August 2023] - Emergency Department Use Across Income Groups Following an Increase in Cost-Sharing
* **Authors/Institution:** Yushan Wu, Dorothy Yingxuan Wang, Shi Zhao, Maggie Haitian Wang, Eliza Lai-Yi Wong, Eng-Kiong Yeoh — CUHK (JC School of Public Health and Primary Care)
* **Type:** Published Peer-Reviewed Paper
* **Link:** https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2808456 (DOI: 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2023.29577)
* **Core Analysis:** This CUHK-led study examined emergency department use across income groups following the June 2017 fee increase from HK$100 to HK$180 per A&E visit, published in JAMA Network Open in August 2023.[[1]](https://hspr.cuhk.edu.hk/resources/)
* **Relevance to Cat 4/5 & Data Presentation:**
    - The statistically significant reduction in urgent ED care, particularly among the low-income group, warrants further study to assess the long-term health impact of the fee increase.[[10]](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Validation-of-the-Hong-Kong-accident-and-emergency-Fan-Leung/b37faefcc9179250cac8c8db6263019454ca51a0) This finding is critical: fee increases (like the recent 2025 move to HK$400) may divert Cat 4/5 patients, but the diversion may disproportionately affect low-income groups who lack awareness of alternatives.
    - A public dashboard should feature alternative care options prominently (with pricing) to help patients—especially those from low-income groups—make informed choices *before* committing to the A&E queue.

* **Authors/Institution:** Eliza Lai-Yi Wong, Annie Wai-Ling Cheung, Hong Qiu, Jonathan Chun-Hei Ma, Eng-Kiong Yeoh — CUHK (Centre for Health Systems and Policy Research)
* **Type:** Published Peer-Reviewed Paper
* **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-023-09560-y
* **Core Analysis:** This paper validates a locally developed instrument for measuring patient experience in A&E departments across Hong Kong's public hospitals, facilitating quality improvement.[[1]](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12913-023-09560-y) The research was conducted at CUHK's Centre for Health Systems and Policy Research under license with the Hong Kong Hospital Authority.[[1]](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12913-023-09560-y)
* **Relevance to Cat 4/5 & Data Presentation:**
    - The validated A&E Experience Questionnaire captures dimensions of patient experience including communication during wait times and perceived quality. A redesigned public dashboard could incorporate crowd-sourced patient experience scores alongside objective wait times, giving Cat 4/5 patients a richer picture of what to expect at each hospital.

### [2022] - A Data-Driven Multi-Fidelity Simulation Optimization for Medical Staff Configuration at an Emergency Department in Hong Kong
* **Authors/Institution:** Hainan Guo, Haobin Gu, Yifan Zhou et al. (Shenzhen University & collaborators)
* **Type:** Published Peer-Reviewed Paper
* **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10696-020-09395-3 (Flexible Services and Manufacturing Journal, 34, 238–262, 2022)
* **Core Analysis:** This study focuses on optimizing the medical staff configuration to alleviate overcrowding. According to the service requirements proposed by the Hong Kong government, 90% of urgent patients should receive treatment within 30 min.[[6]](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10696-020-09395-3)
* **Relevance to Cat 4/5 & Data Presentation:**
    - The simulation model demonstrates how staffing patterns directly impact wait times for lower-acuity patients. Cat 4/5 wait times spike during shifts where staffing is optimized only for Cat 1–3 targets. A predictive public dashboard could incorporate staffing-shift patterns (e.g., showing that wait times typically improve after shift changes at known hours).

### [July 2020] - An Integrated Approach of Machine Learning and Systems Thinking for Waiting Time Prediction in an Emergency Department
* **Authors/Institution:** Yong-Hong Kuo, Nicholas B. Chan, Janny M.Y. Leung, Helen Meng, Anthony Man-Cho So, Kelvin K.F. Tsoi, Colin A. Graham — CUHK (Systems Engineering & Engineering Management / A&E Medicine)
* **Type:** Published Peer-Reviewed Paper
* **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijmedinf.2020.104143
* **Core Analysis:** The study conducted computational experiments based on a dataset collected from an ED in Hong Kong (Prince of Wales Hospital). All four machine learning algorithms with systems knowledge outperformed the baseline model, with three algorithms reducing the mean-square error by approximately 20%.[[1]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1386505619309657)
* **Relevance to Cat 4/5 & Data Presentation:**
    - Accurate patient waiting time prediction through machine learning is useful for hospitals to manage resources. Also, patients can make informed decisions about waiting in the ED or seeking medical services at other healthcare facilities.[[1]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1386505619309657)
    - **Key Insight:** This paper provides the technical blueprint for replacing the current retrospective 95th-percentile HA system with a **personalized, real-time, ML-based predicted waiting time** for each individual patient at each hospital — the most impactful improvement for Cat 4/5 patients.
    - This research is supported by Health and Medical Research Fund, Food and Health Bureau, the Hong Kong SAR Government.[[1]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1386505619309657) Government backing suggests institutional feasibility.

### [June 2020] - Performance of a Three-Level Triage Scale in Live Triage Encounters in an Emergency Department in Hong Kong
* **Authors/Institution:** Rex Pui Kin Lam, Shing Lam Kwok, Vi Ka Chaang, Lujie Chen, Eric Ho Yin Lau, Kin Ling Chan — HKU (Emergency Medicine Unit) / Gleneagles Hong Kong Hospital
* **Type:** Published Peer-Reviewed Paper
* **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1186/s12245-020-00288-8
* **Core Analysis:** The team modified the Australasian Triage Scale and developed a 3-level triage scale, evaluating its validity, reliability, and over- and under-triage rates in real patient encounters.[[1]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32522272/) The 3-level triage system appears to have good validity and reasonable reliability in a low-volume ED setting. Further studies comparing 3-level and prevailing 5-level triage scales in live triage encounters are warranted.[[1]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32522272/)
* **Relevance to Cat 4/5 & Data Presentation:**
    - A simplified triage system could reduce the ambiguity between Cat 3 and Cat 4 that currently plagues the 5-level system. Reducing mis-triage between Cat 3/4 is critical because "We have had to dedicate some resources looking after patients from categories four and five [of less urgent cases], which is why we couldn't tend to some of the [category three patients] within half an hour."[[7]](https://www.scmp.com/news/hong-kong/health-environment/article/3303055/hong-kong-ae-wards-aim-cut-wait-times-urgent-patients-fee-change)
    - Better triage accuracy means more reliable wait-time predictions per category for any public-facing tool.

### [November 2018] - Waiting Time in Public Hospitals: Case Study of Total Joint Replacement in Hong Kong
* **Authors/Institution:** Thomas C.T. Wong — Emerald Publishing (Asia Pacific Journal of Health Management)
* **Type:** Published Peer-Reviewed Paper
* **Link:** https://www.emerald.com/pap/article/21/2/120/322297/
* **Core Analysis:** The average waiting time for semi-urgent and non-urgent patients in 2014–2016 was around 2 hours, and the latest A&E waiting time of Prince of Wales Hospital at mid-night exceeded 8 hours. This reflects that some public hospitals are seriously operating beyond their capacity, leading to long waiting time for non-emergency patients, which constitute over half of the A&E attendance.[[3]](https://www.emerald.com/pap/article/21/2/120/322297/Waiting-time-in-public-hospitals-case-study-of)
* **Relevance to Cat 4/5 & Data Presentation:**
    - Long waiting time in A&E departments should not have occurred if only A&E cases are entertained, as in the case of specialist services.[[3]](https://www.emerald.com/pap/article/21/2/120/322297/Waiting-time-in-public-hospitals-case-study-of) This underscores the structural problem of Cat 4/5 patients entering A&E due to absent primary care alternatives.
    - The over-reliance on public services has resulted in long waiting time in public hospitals in Hong Kong, particularly in the emergency services.[[3]](https://www.emerald.com/pap/article/21/2/120/322297/Waiting-time-in-public-hospitals-case-study-of) A wait-time tool should explicitly display estimated waits for Cat 4/5 separately (not lumped with the general average) to set accurate expectations.

## Supporting Context: Government Policy and Data Sources

For completeness, several government sources confirm the structural context underpinning this research:

- According to the Hospital Authority, 55.6% — or 1,110,242 — of nearly 2 million first-time patients at A&E last year were triaged as semi- or non-urgent cases.[[7]](https://www.scmp.com/news/hong-kong/health-environment/article/3303055/hong-kong-ae-wards-aim-cut-wait-times-urgent-patients-fee-change) (SCMP, March 2025)
- The HA's service targets specify that all Critical patients (100%) receive immediate treatment, and Emergency and Urgent patients are prioritised, with targets that most Emergency (95%) and Urgent (90%) patients will be treated within 15 or 30 minutes respectively.[[8]](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202403/20/P2024032000495.htm) There are **no service targets for Cat 4/5 patients.** (LegCo Q&A, March 2024)
- A revised version of the HA A&E waiting time dataset was released on 13 October 2025 with updated data structure and URLs.[[2]](https://data.gov.hk/en-data/dataset/hospital-hadata-ae-waiting-time) (DATA.GOV.HK)

## <synthesis>

### Three Most Viable Strategies for Improving the Public Wait-Time Dashboard for Category 4/5 Patients

**Strategy 1: Switch from Retrospective 95th-Percentile to Real-Time Predicted Median Wait Times**

The Park et al. (2023, MSOM) study provides the strongest evidence-based case for this change. The current HA system publishes the 95th percentile of wait times from the past 3 hours — a metric that is both overly pessimistic and too stale to be actionable. Their counterfactual analysis demonstrates that reducing the assessment window to 1 hour and using the median (or even a forward-looking predicted WT based on current crowding) would reduce average actual wait times by ~4.6% and patients leaving without being seen by ~8.5%. Kuo et al. (2020, IJMI) at CUHK have already built and validated an ML-based predictive model using Hong Kong ED data that outperforms linear baselines by ~20%, establishing the technical feasibility of deploying personalized, real-time wait-time predictions per hospital, per triage category.

**Strategy 2: Integrate Alternative Care Options Directly Into the Wait-Time Display**

Multiple papers converge on the finding that Cat 4/5 patients overwhelm A&E because of the lack of accessible primary care alternatives, not because their conditions require emergency care. The Springer chapter on A&E misuse (2025) and Wu et al. (2023, JAMA Netw Open) on fee-driven diversion both highlight that patients need to be shown viable alternatives *at the moment of decision*, not just wait times. A redesigned tool should display, alongside each hospital's predicted wait, a map of nearby private GPs, GOPCs, and 24-hour clinics with current availability, pricing, and travel times — effectively making the dashboard a *healthcare navigation tool* rather than a passive information display. The 2024 government data already lists 1,084+ medical practices open in evening hours.

**Strategy 3: Present Category-Specific Wait Times With Hospital Comparison and Temporal Patterns**

Currently, the HA website shows a single wait-time figure per hospital without triage-category breakdown for Cat 4/5. Given that Cat 4/5 patients form 55.6% of all A&E visits (per 2024 HA data) and have no service-level target, their wait experience is invisible in the current system. A new dashboard should show predicted waits *specifically* for Cat 4/5 at each hospital side-by-side, enabling geographic comparison (Park et al. found patients will travel 4.8 km extra to save 1 hour). Incorporating temporal patterns (known from Guo et al. 2022 and Kuo et al. 2020 staffing models) — such as "wait times typically decrease after 10pm" or "avoid Monday mornings" — would give Cat 4/5 patients the ability to time their visit or choose a less crowded hospital, achieving genuine patient diversion without additional infrastructure.

</synthesis>
